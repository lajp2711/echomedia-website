# Voice Cloning Consent Checklist

Use this checklist before cloning, generating, or publishing any AI voice content.

## Identity and Ownership

- [ ] Identify whose voice will be used.
- [ ] Confirm the person is alive, reachable, and able to consent.
- [ ] Confirm whether the voice is personal, employee, contractor, brand, public figure, or fictional/character voice.
- [ ] Confirm no third-party voice rights are being implied.

## Written Consent

- [ ] Obtain written permission before cloning or generating content.
- [ ] Define allowed content categories.
- [ ] Define prohibited content categories.
- [ ] Define allowed platforms.
- [ ] Define who can generate content.
- [ ] Define who can approve publication.
- [ ] Define how consent can be revoked.

## Disclosure

- [ ] Decide when content should disclose AI voice use.
- [ ] Add disclosure language to descriptions, captions, or publishing notes when appropriate.
- [ ] Avoid implying live endorsement when content is generated.
- [ ] Avoid generating sensitive claims, medical advice, legal advice, financial promises, political persuasion, or adult content without stricter review.

## Review and Safety

- [ ] Human-review every generated output.
- [ ] Check tone, accuracy, claims, and context.
- [ ] Confirm the output does not impersonate someone outside approved boundaries.
- [ ] Save the final approved script/audio record.
- [ ] Track publishing date, platform, and owner.

## Revocation and Archival

- [ ] Keep a process for removing content if consent is withdrawn.
- [ ] Archive source scripts and generated files.
- [ ] Track who approved each asset.
- [ ] Review consent terms on a recurring basis.

## Practical Consent Note

For creator and business workflows, keep consent simple, written, and project-specific. When in doubt, do not generate the voice until the rights and review path are clear.
